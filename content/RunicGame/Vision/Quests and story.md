## Main story

* **Mandatory, long and non-intrusive.** The main story is long (corrected on 27 October; earlier notes said short). What it must be is non-intrusive: it runs beside the rest of the leveling and never gets in the way.  
* **Branching choices.** Chapter 1 is per race, in its starting area, and every branch merges at the faction capital. From chapter 2 on, each faction has its own chapters, and they mirror each other: the same story of the world and the war, told from each side, without diverging much. Earlier choices can change later branches.  
* **Regional arcs** with branches are part of the main story. *Proposal: a chapter needs some of its region's arcs (ex.: 2 of 4), in any order; the rest stay optional and still count for completion.*  
* **Story missions** are engaging activities (a dungeon, an event, a naval battle, a heist, a trial), never "kill 50 mobs"; kills only happen inside them. Main story missions have no complex puzzles.  
* **Presentation:** text with still images, like ArcheAge's; every line can be skipped. No voice acting unless the game gets large funding.  
* **Alts and replays:** a Lineage can skip the chapters it has already played, and each chapter keeps all its branches for replay. *Proposal: a replay is a memory relived at an [[Real exploration#Altars, death, respawn and teleports|Altar]], recorded per Lineage.*  
* **One thread:** a single story runs across every expansion. Each Book ends with a big twist that moves the same story on, instead of a new threat each expansion, and the timeline is long, like WoW's.  
* Quests branch like trees; there are group quests and story dungeons along the leveling; no level scaling and no NPC companions.  
* The end of the main story gives the campaign weapon, the 100% reference of the [[Item progression and infinite progression#Power budget|power budget]].

## Quest design

* **Activities, not chores:** quests are involved activities, never "kill X" or "collect X". *Proposal: the default shape is call, gathering with different verbs, a journey that matters, a climax and a return with a consequence and an unlock, after RuneScape's Dragon Slayer.*  
* **Not marked:** quests are not hard to find, but there is never an exclamation mark over an NPC; at most a mark on the compass or a circle on the region map. *Proposal: four guidance levels (0 secret, 1 described, 2 compass, 3 circle), raised by knowledge and charts.*  
* **The Chronicle:** a timeline journal like Divinity: Original Sin 2's and Baldur's Gate 3's: what happened, in order, and what you chose. It points to specific NPCs by name and role, and the player has to find them. Entering a new expansion, chapter or region adds a lead automatically.  
* **The Codex:** knowledge is collected automatically, like Crimson Desert's; players never take notes by hand. Knowledge earns points, and the reward system is designed later. There is no energy system.  
* **Asking NPCs:** like EverQuest's keywords, but through knowledge instead of typed words. Every knowledge entry has tags, every NPC has the tags they know, and an NPC answers only what they would know; the more tags match, the fuller the answer.  
* **Secrets** like World of Warcraft's and EverQuest's: the game never mentions them. A typical secret starts with an item whose description is a riddle, and whoever discovers it becomes a legend.  
* **Quest points**, RuneScape style. The name is open.  
* **Quest items** never take normal inventory space, are never lost, stolen or dropped, and are never PvP targets (see Inventory).  
* **Authoring:** story, quests, places, NPCs, knowledge and items are Markdown notes in the RunicWorld Obsidian vault, in the RunicGame repository, structure first and dialogue last.
