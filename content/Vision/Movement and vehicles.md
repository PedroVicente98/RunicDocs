Exploring happens on foot, mounted, with a glider, with a wing, in vehicles and by ship. Flight exists only in the flight zones: special regions of the neutral continent (for now one, the region of the floating islands) and the Sky Island; elsewhere you can only glide, and over the ocean only with the wind current.

* **Glider (ArcheAge style):** only glides. It is the first way to glide and uses the wind currents of the regions with that gimmick. Gliders are crafted at a table from a design.  
* **Wing:** equipment, equipped and not summoned. It glides like the glider and flies in the flight zones.  
* **Mounts with a function:** gliding and flying are separate capabilities, and a mount can have one, both or neither. There are also mounts that swim and mounts that climb. Function is what makes a mount desirable, not just speed.  
* **Stamina** is its own resource, physical effort, separate from labor: one bar, Dark Souls style, spent by attacks and martial skills, sprinting, climbing, fast swimming, open-sea fishing and moving heavy objects. Costs are flat, the same in and out of combat; consumption and regeneration are fast; maximum stamina and its regeneration are separate stats that come mostly from items and passives, never from the exploration shop. Guard and evasion have their own charges (see [[Combat#Resources|Resources]]).  
* **Climbing**, in the style of Crimson Desert: characters climb large mountains and cliffs with stamina. The terrain sets the drain by difficulty, and gear (spiked shoes, climbing gear) lowers it on the surfaces it covers. Some surfaces can never be climbed (castle and fort walls, barriers, zone boundaries, instance walls and house walls), and structures aren't climbable unless an artist opts them in. Getting hit while climbing removes a fixed part of the stamina. Pitons and ropes are consumables that help everyone on that stretch.  
* **Ship hulls** are climbed only with boarding hooks, which can be cut, the crew ropes every ship has, or movement skills.  
* **Vehicles:** the other vehicles are summoned by scroll. Flying vehicles work only in the flight zones. New vehicles take packs when they are built, except those made at a bench, such as gliders.  
* **Trade packs** don't let you fly or glide, only walk slowly. They need vehicles or specialized mounts; a mount carries one pack.  
* **Ships of several sizes** are essential for trade packs, housing islands and naval raids. Ships are built from designs on a construction platform.  
* **Context kit:** mounted, with a glider or with a wing, the character uses only the context skills (see the Weapon Class System).  
* **Between maps:** how players reach the Sky Island is *being defined*; caves and rivers go down to the Underground, in the same map; other maps use portals.  
* **Every discrete action** (interactions, starting and stopping a sprint, using an item, skills) has a minimum time, the action floor, set per action type (default 0.25 s, adjustable), so starting a sprint never blocks a skill pressed right after. It prevents spam in a highly interactive world.  
* **Ships** have two layers: parts (hull, sails, helm, keel) that can be upgraded, and stations in slots. Every ship has at least one slot and bigger ships have more; slots are categorized by position (broadside, deck, hold, bow and stern), and each station lists the positions it fits, so a cargo hold can't replace a cannon. A trader comes with trade pack slots that can be removed for something else. Every station and trade pack weighs something, each ship has a tonnage capacity, and more weight means less acceleration and turning. There is no minimum crew, but one person can't sail a big ship efficiently. A removed station becomes a crate that can go to another ship. *Placeholder slots: small boat 1 deck; clipper 2 broadside, 1 deck, 1 hold; trader 2 broadside, 1 deck, 3 hold; galleon 8 broadside, 2 deck, 2 hold, plus bow and stern.*
