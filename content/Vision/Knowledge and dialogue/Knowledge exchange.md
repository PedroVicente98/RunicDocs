Telling an NPC what you know is the main conversation mechanic. It buys standing, not knowledge: standing opens the deeper answers (see [[NPC knowledge and trust#Trust and conditions|Trust and conditions]]).

### How it works

* The character offers one piece of knowledge at a time. The NPC reacts, and a good offer raises the character's standing with that NPC.  
* **Per character, not world-wide.** The NPC doesn't pass the knowledge on, and every character makes the same trades with it, as in any MMORPG where everyone does the same quests. A quest teaches the mechanic.  
* **Value = relevance × depth × novelty**, hidden until the NPC reacts. Relevance comes from the NPC's interests, a section of its profile. Novelty is zero for anything the NPC already knows.

### Keeping it from becoming trivial

* **Bad offers cost patience:** something outside the NPC's interests, or something it already knows. Offering everything ends the conversation quickly (see [[Conversations#Patience|Patience]]).  
* **Each piece counts once per NPC.**  
* **Common knowledge is worth nothing.** Standing comes from Expertise and Hidden entries, which are scarce.  
* **Wants:** each key NPC is after 1 to 3 specific entries and hints at them in dialogue. Bringing one gives the biggest gain, so trading becomes a small hunt.  
* **Trade cap:** each NPC sets how far trades can raise standing (`trade_cap: Trusted`). The top tier needs that NPC's quest or story.  
* **Secrets have a price:** telling one lowers reputation with the organization it belongs to.  
* The [[NPC knowledge web]] shows what you both know, so you can tell what not to offer.  
* *Numbers: the trades made are (NPC, entry) pairs per character, at most a few thousand × 6 bytes ≈ 12 KB.*
