### Series and story

Decided on 5 November 2026 (see [[RunicGame — Design Compile, 5 to 9 November 2026]]); the details live in [[RunicGame — Quest Design]] and [[RunicGame — Lore]].

* **No main story.** The game is non-linear, like RuneScape: series, quest lines and contained quests. A quest can be linear inside; the game isn't. No quest is mandatory, but the rewards draw players in. The old main story (the faction's continent and the war) becomes one very long faction series of grandmaster complexity, which needs faction reputation, quest points and level. Low-level quests never involve the player in the big picture, and there is no world thread through every expansion.  
* **Prerequisites:** only series have quest prerequisites. Other quests can require level, reputation, professions, knowledge or quest points, never another quest.  
* **Starts** are spread evenly across the map, so every level has starts in several regions.  
* **Beginner quests** stay inside each race's region; a series around levels 8 to 12 mixes the faction's races. Visitors can do another race's quests, with the pronoun changed and an opening line about being a visitor; their rewards stay useful later (inventory slots, the race's mount), and their difficulty comes from verbs, not combat.  
* **Faction quests** come in mirrored pairs: one structure, two settings, with small changes per faction. Organization quests are the same for everyone.  
* **Story missions** are engaging activities (a dungeon, an event, a naval battle, a heist, a trial), never "kill 50 mobs"; kills only happen inside them.  
* **Presentation:** text with still images, like ArcheAge's; every line can be skipped. No voice acting unless the game gets large funding.  
* Quests branch like trees; there are group quests and story dungeons along the leveling; no level scaling and no NPC companions.  
* **Rewards** come in categories: access, travel, professions, housing, exploration, prestige, plus a special category. A quest never gives an item that can be lost for good, such as a weapon; quests give recipes, consumables, currency, unlocks, experience, reputation and quest points. Epic quests give at most epic recipes for combat, never combat skills. The faction series gives a weapon recipe that slightly beats the reference weapon.  
* **Job contracts:** a quest that needs a profession item can be completed through a job contract. Profession quests are the exception: they can't be outsourced and give profession XP and their own currency.  
* **Event quests** are the only repeatable quests, and any event can be given as a quest.  
* *Numbers: about 30 quests per faction and 140 shared, toward a goal of 250 or more, built a few at a time.*

### Quest design

* **Activities, not chores:** quests are involved activities, never "kill X" or "collect X". *Proposal: the default shape is call, gathering with different verbs, a journey that matters, a climax and a return with a consequence and an unlock, after RuneScape's Dragon Slayer.*  
* **Not marked:** quests are not hard to find, but there is never an exclamation mark over an NPC; at most a mark on the compass or a circle on the region map. *Proposal: four guidance levels (0 secret, 1 described, 2 compass, 3 circle), raised by knowledge and charts.*  
* **The NPC rune:** an NPC with something new for this character (a quest, a hint, a vendor or knowledge) turns its head and shows a small rune on its nameplate, only within a few meters; it disappears once everything new has been heard. Secrets never show it unless the player carries what the NPC reacts to, and background NPCs never show it.  
* **The quest helper** keeps only hints, the knowledge linked to the quest, the timeline and, in some cases, the area around an object.  
* **Overhear:** a player who stays in an area for the zone's set time (e.g. 1 to 3 s) gets a knowledge entry or a lead, once per character, with optional requirements such as an active quest or a prior entry.  
* **The Chronicle:** a timeline journal like Divinity: Original Sin 2's and Baldur's Gate 3's: what happened, in order, and what you chose. It points to specific NPCs by name and role, and the player has to find them. Entering a new expansion, chapter or region adds a lead automatically.  
* **The Codex:** knowledge is collected automatically, like Crimson Desert's; players never take notes by hand. Knowledge earns points, and the reward system is designed later. There is no energy system.  
* **Asking NPCs:** like EverQuest's keywords, but through knowledge instead of typed words. Every knowledge entry has tags with tiers, every NPC knows tags through their role, home and faction, and an NPC answers only what they would know; the more tags match, the fuller the answer.  
* **Secrets** like World of Warcraft's and EverQuest's: the game never mentions them. A typical secret starts with an item whose description is a riddle, and whoever discovers it becomes a legend.  
* **Quest points**, RuneScape style, with a regional part that counts toward that region's reputation. The name is open.  
* **Quest items** never take normal inventory space, are never lost, stolen or dropped, and are never PvP targets (see [[Inventory, equipment and the Market Isle#Inventory|Inventory]]).  
* **Authoring:** story, quests, places, NPCs, knowledge and items are Markdown notes in the RunicWorld Obsidian vault, in the RunicGame repository, structure first and dialogue last.
